# The four things you can change

Every brewing method, from a ₹500 French press to a ₹2 lakh espresso machine, is the same four variables. Learn these and you can brew on anything.

## 1. Ratio

How much coffee to how much water, by weight.

**Start at 1:16** — 60g of coffee per litre, or about **15g of coffee to 250ml of water** for a single mug. That's a good filter-coffee ratio and a sane default.

- Want it stronger? Go to 1:15 or 1:14.
- Want it lighter? 1:17.
- Espresso is a different world entirely: roughly 1:2 — 18g in, 36g out.

**Use a scale.** This is the cheapest upgrade in coffee, around ₹800, and it does more for consistency than any gadget. A "tablespoon" of coffee varies by 40% depending on the bean and how you scoop.

## 2. Grind size

The main dial you'll actually turn. Finer grind, more surface area, faster extraction.

| Method | Grind | Rough feel |
|---|---|---|
| Espresso | Very fine | Powdered sugar |
| Moka pot | Fine | Fine sand |
| South Indian filter | Fine–medium | Just past sand |
| AeroPress | Medium–fine | Table salt |
| Pourover / V60 | Medium | Coarse sand |
| French press | Coarse | Breadcrumbs |

These are starting points, not laws. Adjust based on how the coffee tastes, not what a chart says.

## 3. Water temperature

**Aim for 92–96°C.** In practice: boil the kettle, take the lid off, wait 30 seconds.

Hotter water extracts faster. Light roasts are dense and generally want water at the top of that range; dark roasts are brittle and already extract easily, so cooler water (around 88–90°C) keeps them from turning bitter.

You don't need a variable-temperature kettle to start. You do eventually want a **gooseneck kettle** for pourover — not for temperature, but because the narrow spout is the only way to control where the water actually lands.

## 4. Time

How long water and coffee stay in contact.

- Pourover: 2½–3½ minutes total
- AeroPress: 1–2 minutes
- French press: 4 minutes
- Espresso: 25–30 seconds

Time and grind are linked: if your pourover is finishing way too fast, the grind is too coarse; if it's stalling and taking five minutes, it's too fine.

## Water itself

The thing nobody mentions: **your cup is 98% water**, and Delhi tap water is hard enough to genuinely hurt it. Heavy mineral content mutes acidity and flattens flavour, and it will scale up a kettle or espresso machine fast.

You don't need laboratory water. Bottled low-TDS water, or an RO filter with some mineral content added back, is a bigger improvement than most people expect — often more noticeable than switching beans. Fully distilled or zero-TDS RO water is the one thing to avoid: with no minerals at all, there's nothing to pull flavour out of the coffee and the result tastes hollow.
