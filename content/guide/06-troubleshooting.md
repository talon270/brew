# When it tastes bad, read this

Almost every bad cup is one of two problems. Learning to tell them apart is the single most useful skill in home brewing, because the fixes are opposites — and guessing wrong makes it worse.

## Sour, sharp, thin, empty finish

**You under-extracted.** Not enough came out of the grounds. People often read this as "too acidic" and blame the bean, but it's usually the brew.

The tell: it hits sharp at the front, then vanishes. Nothing lingers. Sometimes a bit salty.

Fix any one of these:

- **Grind finer** — the biggest lever, try this first
- Brew longer
- Hotter water
- Stir or agitate more during the brew

## Bitter, harsh, dry, drying

**You over-extracted.** Too much came out, including the harsh compounds that dissolve last.

The tell: it lingers unpleasantly and dries your mouth out, like over-steeped tea.

Fix any one of these:

- **Grind coarser**
- Brew shorter
- Cooler water
- Agitate less

## Change one thing at a time

The rule that actually gets you to good coffee: change **one** variable, brew again, taste. Change three at once and you learn nothing, because you don't know which one did it.

Grind size is the strongest lever, so make that your first move nearly every time.

## Both at once?

Sour *and* bitter in the same cup — sharp up front, harsh at the back — usually means **uneven grind**, not a bad recipe. Fine particles are over-extracting while coarse ones under-extract, simultaneously. No recipe change fixes this.

That's a grinder problem, and it's the reason [the grinder comes first](#).

## Flat, dull, cardboard-like

Nothing wrong with your technique — the coffee is **stale**. Check the roast date. Past about a month from roasting, this is what you get, and there's no brewing around it.

## It's fine, but I don't enjoy it

Perfectly legitimate, and different from all of the above. A well-brewed light Ethiopian natural is objectively a good cup of coffee and you're still allowed to dislike it.

Retake the taste quiz honestly rather than aspirationally — answer as the person who drinks the coffee, not the person you think a coffee enthusiast is supposed to be. The recommendations are only as good as that input.
