# Buy a grinder before anything else

If you take one thing from this guide: **the grinder matters more than the coffee machine, and it isn't close.**

## Why

Brewing is extraction — hot water dissolving compounds out of coffee grounds. How fast that happens depends almost entirely on the size of the particles. Small particles give up their flavour quickly; large ones take longer.

So if your grind is uneven, you don't get an average cup. You get two cups in one glass: the dust over-extracts into something bitter and drying, the boulders under-extract into something sour and thin, and you drink both at once. No amount of technique fixes it. You cannot brew your way out of a bad grind.

This is why a ₹6,000 hand grinder with a ₹2,000 French press makes better coffee than a ₹40,000 espresso machine with a ₹1,500 blade grinder. It's not a close call, and it's the single most common expensive mistake beginners make.

## Blade grinders

A blade grinder is a propeller in a cup. It doesn't grind, it smashes — and it keeps smashing whatever is already small enough while the big pieces bounce around untouched. The output is dust and boulders by design.

They're sold everywhere, they're cheap, and they will cap how good your coffee can be no matter what else you buy. If you own one, replacing it is the highest-value upgrade available to you.

## Burr grinders

Burrs are two cutting surfaces a set distance apart. Coffee passes through and can only exit once it's small enough to fit the gap — so everything comes out roughly the same size. Change the gap, change the grind size.

- **Conical burrs** — a cone inside a ring. Common, efficient, forgiving.
- **Flat burrs** — two parallel rings. Tend to produce a narrower particle range, which reads in the cup as more clarity and separation between flavours.

Both are good. The jump from blade to burr is enormous; the jump from conical to flat is a refinement you'll notice later.

## Hand or electric?

In India, hand grinders are dramatically better value. Electric grinders are mostly imported and carry duty, so a ₹24,000 electric grinder often grinds no better than a ₹9,000 hand grinder — you're paying for the motor and the convenience, not the cup.

Buy electric if you make several coffees a day, have people to serve, or know yourself well enough to admit you'll skip coffee rather than crank a handle at 7am. Otherwise start manual.

## The espresso exception

Espresso needs a much finer grind than any other method, and needs to adjust in tiny increments — the difference between a good shot and a bad one can be a fraction of a turn. Many grinders that are excellent for pourover simply **cannot go fine enough**, or have adjustment steps too coarse to land on the right setting.

This is why "best grinder under ₹10,000" has no single answer. If you brew pourover, the Timemore C3 is a great call. If you pull espresso, the same grinder will frustrate you daily. Tell the app how you brew and it'll filter accordingly — that's the entire point of the grinder finder.
