# Buying beans, and keeping them alive

## Look for a roast date, not an expiry date

An expiry date on coffee is close to meaningless — coffee doesn't spoil, it fades. A roast date tells you what you need to know.

Coffee is at its best roughly **5 to 21 days after roasting**. Immediately after roasting it's still venting CO₂, which physically gets in the way of the water and makes brews taste thin and unpredictable. After about a month, the aromatics have largely gone and you're drinking a flatter, duller version of what you paid for.

If a bag has no roast date, that's the roaster telling you it doesn't matter to them. Believe them.

## Buy small, buy often

250g is the right unit. It's about two weeks of daily coffee for one person, which is exactly the window where the coffee is good.

A kilo bag is worse value than it looks, because the last 400g of it will taste stale. Buying smaller and more often costs slightly more per gram and gets you noticeably better coffee, which is a trade worth making.

## Whole bean, always

Pre-ground coffee starts losing aromatics within minutes — the grinding is what exposes all that surface area to air. A bag of pre-ground coffee is stale before you open it, and it's ground to one guessed setting that probably doesn't suit your brewer.

If you don't have a grinder yet, this is another argument for [buying one first](#).

## Storing it

Keep it simple:

- **Airtight container, room temperature, out of the light.** The bag it came in is usually fine if it has a one-way valve and you roll it down.
- **Not the fridge.** Coffee is porous and will take on the smell of everything else in there. Worse, every time you take it out, condensation forms on cold beans.
- **The freezer works, but only properly.** Freezing in small sealed portions you never re-open genuinely preserves coffee for months. Repeatedly opening one bag in the freezer is worse than leaving it on the counter.

In Delhi specifically: summer heat and monsoon humidity both accelerate staling. A sealed container in a cupboard away from the stove is doing more work here than it would in a milder climate.

## Where to buy in Delhi NCR

Roasters based in or near NCR mean fresher coffee and faster delivery — often roasted-to-order and with you in two or three days:

- **Blue Tokai** — Delhi-based, the widest range, reliable, roast dates on everything. The easiest starting point.
- **Savorworks** — Noida. Strong single origins and a well-regarded espresso blend.
- **Quick Brown Fox** — Delhi. Approachable blends, good for milk drinks.
- **Grey Soul** — Gurgaon.
- **Devans** — Delhi, long-established.

Buying from further afield (Corridor Seven in Nagpur, KC Roasters and Subko in Mumbai, Naivo, Curious Life) is completely fine — most roast to order and ship in a few days, which still beats anything sitting on a supermarket shelf.
